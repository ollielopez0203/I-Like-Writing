There is a particular quiet that only exists in the hours before a move. The boxes are taped, the walls are bare, and the apartment stops feeling like yours somewhere around the second-to-last trip to the car.

{% media "image", "/images/placeholder-1.jpg", "The last morning, empty apartment" %}

I took this photo the morning I left. Nothing dramatic about it — just light coming through a window that no longer had curtains — but I keep coming back to it when I try to explain why the move felt bigger than it should have.

There's a passage I kept thinking about that week, from a book I'd read years earlier and mostly forgotten:

{% media "quote", "You don't leave a place. A place leaves you, a little at a time, starting the moment you decide to go.", "Some Book, p.114" %}

I'm not sure I agree with it, exactly. But it was the right sentence at the right time, which is sometimes all a sentence needs to do.
