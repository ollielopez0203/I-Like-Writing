There are these fighters on Instagram that keep popping up in my feed. They have these specific styles of fighting, styles that are particularly highlighted by the manner in which they shadowbox, which angers me quite a bit. What is so frustrating is that they really aren't doing anything wrong. They are certainly talented; that is undeniable. I think there are many qualities that one should try and take from their manners that would prove a great improver of one's own abilities. But still, they vex me somehow. I am vexed. 

I have narrowed it down. It is this: their styles are unrealistic and over the top. In a word, excessive describes it pretty well. The drunk style, if we're getting specific, involves moving in very exaggerated and fluid motions that are unorthodox to the opponent, and rather awkward in certain cases if you are standing with the user toe-to-toe. They act drunk, in short. The hands are down, the shoulders are down, the chin is exposed. It is all an insult to the traditional guard we are brought up on. But it works, in certain instances, precisely because it is so awkward to watch take place in front of you as the opponent. I liken the awkwardness to fighting a capoeira specialist, because in many ways it is just that: a dance. But it is, above all, really annoying to watch when it becomes the default manner of a fighter's style. And it becomes default all too easily: as the fighter pulls off more and more fluid deflections, attacks, and evasions using the style, so they begin to adopt it as their natural set of moves. And then they get their fucking chin taken out by someone who doesn't blink, who is unfazed, and who reacts appropriately to the style. I have seen this in sparring, competitions, and online. I have to say here that it isn't the fault of the drunkard, because after all, they have just adopted what worked for them. But it does not help when you get these laymen in comment sections who drool excessively over the style, and exhaust all the compliments in their arsenal to commend the drunkard's manoeuvres. That is really what I am annoyed with. 

Besides the drunk style, which really only comes out in sparring and competition, this other style is something that enrages me quite a lot more. I do not know the name of it. I can only describe it as an exagerrated pantomime of Luke Lessei's Muay Thai style. If you know Muay Thai, perhaps you know what I speak of. It is those hyper fluid movements between elbows and knees, where every finished move puts you in position for another immediately. It really brings out the Art side of the Martial Art, because it is extremely rhythmic, relaxed and smooth. But taken to its extremes, as some decide to do, it looks ridiculous. The hands take this odd shape of an origami crane's folded head, which is not wrong in and of itself, but is just so exagerrated as to be unserious. The elbows fly in these bizarre motions that are visually appealing but have no grounding in reality. The knees bend beautifully, but do not move with power. the teeps flick fast, but with no intentional power behind them.

I am sorry, but the style is too much for me. A good sweep and your nonsense would be over. Those elbows that fly from every which way in a seven-move combination will not get you far after i have thrown a heavy few kicks in you direction, and teeped you back into reality. The style does not survive the fight. It is beautiful, it is mimpressive; it is not real. Looking at the greatest Muay Femur fighters, even they do not move like this. It is all performance for a layman audience, who once again flock to the comment sections and lay it on thick. And it is always something that makes your whole body shudder with awkwardness: "No one is survivng that," "Those elbows!!!" "Muay Thai is different...". Mate, leave it out. Please. I love this sport, and encourage anyone and everyone to participate. But do not gas these styles that are so fundamentally against the core principle of the art. This is the art of stand-up fighting. It was born of Muay Boran, taught to the soldiers of Thailand as their manner of hand-to-hand combat. It has been refined into the ultimate method of taking one's body, and developing every limb and every movement into a violenbt weapon. If takedowns were not a thing, the style of fighting Muay Thai would be impervious.

I am so sure this is not felt by others. Some may call me a gatekeeper, or that I am making a problem out of nothing. 'Live and let live' would be the general feeling, I imagine. They are right. But this is my blog so I don't care (hahaha). Training in Thailand, my trainers made fun of me for my grunts I made when throwing a combination. To be fair, it was rather nasally. They mocked me all through camp, and all through the lead up to my first fight. I fought, was split open, won, and they continued on with the mockery the next day. WHen the cut got infected, they made fun of me all the same. I have no doubt they would look at these styles and say to one another, "What the actual fuck".
