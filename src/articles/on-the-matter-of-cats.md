For my father's birthday, my family decided a cat was in order. This way, we solved two problems that had brought us serious concern: a) the need to buy a substantial gift for our loving dad, and b) bringing a cat into the family fold in direct contradiction to Dad's wishes. We knew this method was a guaranteed success: you see, we had used the same strategy to bring two dogs into our household in prior years.

So, to the great disappointment of our father, we now had a cat in the house. I have since noticed a great many things about this peculiar animal. And whilst I am not the first to notice these things, I would like to get them down here, in order that I can free my mind of them. 

The cat is a regal pet; of all of them I think the most. There is this disdain that they have for you, the owner, that you feel almost instantly when eye contact is made. Their narrow and sharpened pupils gloss over you as they scan the room for their next purpose, as if you were another article of the household. And yet they put up with you, because after all, they must allow you the honour of feeding them, and on the occasion changing their chambers. 

No matter the type, the cat's coat is exquisite. They know this, and take their time to ensure it retains its glamour and brilliance. They take their time, for everything must remain silky and perfect. The coat is only outdone in its splendour by the many layers of pattern and colouration within their iris. No matter the cat in question, we all know of this iris and its depth. There is the blue here, in this part, and the green in this other, and no two regions are the same. They do, however, blot together beautifully to create a masterful gradient. Almost as if they had a hand in concocting such detail, the cat is rather smug about this.

Everything is symmetrical when it comes to the cat. The coat, its striations, the fur, the button-nose, the spread of whiskers on either side: it is all perfect. The cat knows this, just as it knows its superiority. 

A cat owner is an oxymoronic term, given what we understand about them. Perhaps a better term would be keeper, although even that suggests some sort of obedience the cat pays us. We have our duties, as keepers of the cat: as I already said, the food and drink, and the litter, must all be maintained without fail. The duties of the cat in question? Perhaps their only such labour is the duty to cope with their keepers. They let you know that this is a laborious task for them, however; in fact, they never fail to inform you of this.

From time to time, the cat will permit you to pat it. It does so at its own annoyance, for it is a gracious being, but there is tact behind this manoeuvre. The feeling you get from stroking a cat eclipses all else. In that fleeting moment, the warm elation of a good pet carries that same quiet flattery and vanity of a compliment being received in respectable company by a person you admire. For that short while, the cat has shown you favour, and you sit there with a reserved pride, for you have conquered the unconquerable, and the cat prefers you to the rest. It then gets up, bored (for you have bored it), and moves on to its next item of the day.

One of the most curious phenomena of the cat is its capacity to find the most ostensibly uncomfortable of locations, and make a bed of it. Even now as I type this, the cat of our house has found her way into one of my mother's treasured plant pots. Whilst you know that this position of rest that the cat has found can't possibly be comfortable, there it will lie for the next few hours, proving quite the contrary. 

The cat is a most efficient creature. Beyond its impressive physical abilities and flexibility, it would rather not show any emotion or change in state unless it is absolutely critical and of the moment to do so. Come the end of days, my cat will still be asleep, comfortably, in its newly usurped plant pot.

There are many more items that deserve our attention when it comes to the cat, and perhaps I shall write a second piece extending these thoughts. I will end here, however, for the moment being.
