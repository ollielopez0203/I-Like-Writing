Here's the thing about toxic masculinity: it looks good. It is attractive, it is strong, it is the man's man; at least that is what many men have been convinced of. If one were to think of toxic masculinity, the mind more likely than not gravitates to the likes of Andrew Tate, or one of his contemporaries. A cheap stereotype, but I think an honest one. And yes, he is a fuckwit––but he is also strong, powerful, rich, muscular, and a man with no short supply of women at his disposal (literally). So we can play the game of calling out the imbecile within, but at the same time in doing so, we reaffirm the physical image of what toxic masculinity is. And boy, is that a snare of resounding success. Indeed many men know of Tate's nonsense, but I can guarantee they resonate with certain characteristics of his.

Our cultures have long imbued men with the desire for strength, for stoicism and leadership. They want muscle. They want attention, and they want sex. They want power. This is not necessarily a good thing, but we must be adults and recognise this is the case if we want to do something about the trouble. \
\
Men have long been promised the world. They've been promised affection and love and the subservience of women to them. Some have grown out of that infantile desire, but some still want it bad. They want their promise kept. It is of no difference to men, young men especially, what package that promise comes in, as long as it comes along all the same.

So, when along comes a man with it all, with the power to be and do as he pleases, so what if he denigrates women? So what if he is an arsehole of the first order? He has found the ends, and therefore he knows the means. He will fulfil the promise men have been convinced of. And so he becomes a tutor to the men of the world. 



What needs to be done --- reverse the stigma of empathetic masculinity = weak, fat, feminine men. We need fighters, bodybuilders, ceos, etc.
